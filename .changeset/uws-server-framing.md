A response that declares its own `Content-Length` goes out with exactly one framing header. The adapter no longer forwards `content-length` or `transfer-encoding` to uWS, which frames every response itself: a body in memory gets uWS's own `Content-Length` instead of a duplicate, a HEAD answer or a 304 without a body (serve-static's HEAD included) reports the declared length as its `Content-Length` and keeps uWS's idle timeout for the connection, a HEAD answer that declares none gets no `Content-Length`, a 204 gets none at all (RFC 9110 forbids it there), any other response without a body is an empty one with `Content-Length: 0` instead of a declared length nothing follows, and a streamed body with a declared length (serve-static files) goes out through `tryEnd()` under that one `Content-Length`, paced by backpressure, instead of `Content-Length` next to `Transfer-Encoding: chunked`. A stream that turns out shorter or longer than declared, or errors, closes the connection rather than ending a wrong frame. A streamed body without a declared length stays chunked and goes out at the pace of the client; a source that errors closes the connection there too, rather than ending the chunked body as if it were complete, and a client that goes away cancels the source at once, even while the source has nothing to send, so a server-sent events producer that has gone quiet stops too. While a response waits for a slow client the socket keeps reading, and a request body nobody reads is given up once more than twice `bodyHighWaterMark` of it has piled up. A response that fails halfway through being written (`fetch` resolving to something that is not a `Response`) closes the connection instead of leaving the client waiting.
